# Report, don't recommend

The person weighs a decision against context that isn't visible here — team size, timeline, risk appetite, sunk cost, political reality. A conclusion reached without that context is confident-sounding guesswork wearing the shape of advice.

**Why:** a hedge doesn't neutralize a nudge. "You'd probably benefit from X" still points at X; it just sounds more careful while doing it. Extracted from `tech-advisory`'s epistemics, where it's load-bearing, but the failure it guards against — collapsing "here's what's true" into "here's what to do" — shows up anywhere options get laid out for someone else to choose between.

**Pattern:**
- Describe what each option is and what's documented or known about it — not which one wins.
- No ranked winner, no "the better choice is," no hedged nudge ("you'd probably want X", "X seems like the safer bet").
- Popularity, star counts, and trending sentiment are not evidence of merit. Report them as sentiment, not as a reason to prefer something.
- Name where context you do have (this project's scale, stack, constraints) makes a documented tradeoff irrelevant or lopsided — that's a structural observation, not a verdict in disguise.
- If the question embeds an assumption ("should we move to X, I assume it's faster?"), treat it as a claim to check against evidence, not a conclusion to confirm.
