# Alternatives

Used only by the `compare-options` playbook — "what else is out there for X", "how are other teams solving this problem", "what are the alternatives to Y".

## What this angle covers

Competing or adjacent tools, libraries, frameworks, patterns, or techniques that solve substantially the same problem as the topic. For each one found, apply the same lens the other three angles use, briefly: what's officially documented about it, any known issues, and what sentiment looks like. The goal is a short, accurate sketch of each option, not a full investigation of every one — depth stays with the original topic.

**No bake-off, no scorecard, no ranking.** List what exists and what's known about each; let the person do their own comparison with context this skill doesn't have.

## How to search it

```text
"<technology>" alternatives
"<technology>" vs <competitor>
best <category of technology> <year>
"<problem the technology solves>" tools OR libraries
```

Cross-check any "X vs Y" writeup's date and whether the author has a stake in one side (a maintainer of one option writing about why it beats the other is a different kind of source than a neutral practitioner's comparison — label it as such, don't discard it).

## Output note

In the investigator's Findings section, group by alternative (one sub-entry per competing option) rather than mixing all alternatives into one list — the synthesizer needs to keep them distinguishable when writing "Alternatives Worth Knowing About".
